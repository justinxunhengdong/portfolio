# Playside Mobile
### Data-informed design and rapid development

I was placed in the mobile team in an initiative to revive the mobile back catalogue, assessing the company's most successfully performing mobile games, maintaining their performance and modernising them where possible. This involved switching between 2-3 mobile projects at a time, dedicating a few days at most to the design of a feature, while juggling the ongoing development and iteration of other features, and assessing reference titles.

These projects included:
* _Garbage Truck 3D_, a hypercasual vehicle sim,
* _Candy Critters_, a merge/tycoon game, 
* _Dumb Ways to Die_, a microgame collection,

This encouraged a lean working process that included forming a hypothesis, describing and detailing the design feature, outlining the rationale with data, and forming a strategy for its validation, detailing player cohorts, A/B tests, and analytics hooks. For smaller features, I often eschewed documentation entirely and instead relied on Slack threads to tag the relevant developers, and developing and assigning acceptance criteria. I found this constant context switching an enjoyable challenge, and found it rewarding to execute upon large and small features asynchronously and validate hypotheses through data.

I also became more deeply involved within data design, pushing for database access to write or generate my own queries, thereby relying less on the data team's limited time. I was able to provide crucial design and programming context, discovering gaps in information and finding misleading data hooks. This most frequently came in the form of adjusting difficulty, where we would measure the differences in player cohorts and adjust values to iron out pain points.

I advocated for updates in the ad monetisation strategies, as many of these decade-old games relied on interstitials, which paradoxically provided most of the game's revenue, but contributed to its churn. We aimed to find the perfect balance of rewarded versus interstitial videos to maintain the game's profitability, minimising player frustration, and adjusting the game's metaprgoression. I enjoyed working in this space, as player or user psychology has been an interest of mine, having previously studied as a UX designer.
