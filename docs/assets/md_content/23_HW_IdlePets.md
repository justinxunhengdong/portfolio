# Horizon Worlds: IdlePets!
### A tycoon game and tech demo
_LOGO OR VIDEO_

_IdlePets!_ was a project I helped design, and was a change of pace to other Meta Horizon projects I had worked on previously. First, it relied _Meta Horizon Studio_, a new standalone proprietary engine made by Meta. Second, it was intended to be used to demo Meta's AI capabilities. This meant we needed to build a game that was extensible and modular enough to describe to an LLM, be flexible enough to handle user asset requests, such as changing meshes, audio and mechanics, and finding a way to demonstrate this in the most flattering way possible. The short deadline between starting development and the intended demo date was around 4 months.

_ECONOMY SC_

I designed the economy and meta, analysing, simplifying and reverse-engineering our reference title, creating the simulation spreadsheet, and coordinating with programmers to implement certain systems, with an eye for flexiblity and extensibility. To simulate the pace of the game, I made a Unity mockup that mimicked and simplified the topology of the game to a demo; we could then answer questions such as "How many clicks is too many clicks?". We also had player upgrades that didn't easily feed into an income projection, such as player speed. I designed the missions and balancing to abstract out all upgrades into a set of points, such as "player collection efficiency". As the long-term status of the project was unknown, I also focused on making the spreadsheet systems polished enough to hand off to a different designer.  

_ART IMG_

I worked with the art team to help design the appearance of the pets, which were originally drones. This involved iterating upon the shape of the drones in 3D to make their appearance visible from a distance, defining their size, personality and relative value. I also defined the level metrics, working with level artists to solve occlusion issues, which was especially important a client request necessitated an immediate pivot into a portrait, one-handed interaction mode.  

The IdlePets project was used to demonstrate Meta Horizon Studio's integrated LLM capabilities at several Meta events across the US and UK. I was briefly involved with the iteration and testing process for this, converting the design documents into an LLM-friendly markdown format, battle-testing different "golden prompts" that would display a healthy range of LLM capabilities, and adjusting context documents to reduce error rates. I coordinated this with a small strike team of developers, providng player advocacy and game systems knowledge. 

Personal opinions of generative AI aside, it was a fascinating opportunity to understand how AI skills were developed, and develop at the bleeding-edge of new technology, and I was proud to work on a project that was demoed across the globe.
